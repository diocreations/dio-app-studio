# Owner usability test — moderator script

Date prepared: 22 November 2026

Status: **prepared, not executed with a real business owner.** Nothing in this
document is a usability result. Automated browser checks are not evidence that
an owner can use Plattr.

## Purpose

Answer one question: can a non-technical owner, with no live assistance,
understand what Plattr read, correct it, choose a concept, make the app
publish-ready and preview it independently?

This is a formative test. Watching four owners is enough to find blocking
confusion; it is not a statistical benchmark.

## Participants

- **Four owners**, one from each pilot trade: retail, restaurant, salon,
  professional services.
- The owner must be the person who would actually run the app, not a marketing
  agency or a technically confident employee standing in for them.
- Recruit **two reserves** in case a session is cancelled.
- Exclude anyone who has worked on Plattr or has seen the new review screens.
- Record role, business size, devices used and confidence with apps on a 1–5
  scale. Do not record passwords, customer data or full contact details in the
  research notes.

## Session setup

- 45–60 minutes, one participant and one moderator. A second observer may take
  notes and must stay silent.
- Use the participant's own phone for the mobile tasks, and a laptop for the
  desktop check if they normally use one.
- The private project link and the owner key are delivered separately. Read the
  key from a password manager into the login field, or let the owner type it;
  never put the key in chat, email, a URL or the recording.
- Use only a clean pilot project. No live payments, no real customer personal
  data, no public indexing.
- Record the screen only with explicit consent. Otherwise record notes and
  timings by hand.
- The moderator does not point at links, name the next step, or explain what a
  label means unless the participant is genuinely stuck. Every intervention is
  logged.

## Opening script

> Thank you for helping. We are testing the product, not you. I want to see
> whether the app explains itself without me. Please think aloud — say what you
> are looking for and what you expect to happen. I will not answer “where do I
> click?” unless you are completely stuck. There is no wrong answer, and you can
> stop at any time. May I record the screen and take notes?

Confirm consent before opening the project.

## Tasks and timings

Start the stopwatch for each task. Use `owner-usability-log.csv` for the raw
numbers.

### Task 0 — Sign in and first impression (not scored)

Open the private project link. Ask the participant to sign in with the owner key.
Let them look at the workspace for 30 seconds without speaking.

Ask:

- “In your own words, what is this page for?”
- “What do you think you need to do next?”

Log whether they find the **Before you approve** checklist without help.

### Task 1 — What did Plattr discover? (discovery review)

Ask the participant to open **What we read** and explain back:

1. What Plattr says it found.
2. Which facts came from their website.
3. Which things Plattr is unsure about.
4. Which things are missing.

Do not prompt with the headings. If they cannot distinguish found, uncertain and
missing after two minutes, that is a critical comprehension failure.

**Measure:** time to correct summary; number of items they miss; number of
moderator corrections.

### Task 2 — Choose a concept

The four pilot projects were generated before this session. In seeded mode, the
moderator opens the three private concept previews from the untracked comparison
sheet generated by `bun scripts/pilot-concept-sheet.mjs`
(`.chrome-qa/pilot-concepts.html`) and starts the timer when the first preview is
revealed.

Ask:

> Which of these three would you want your customers to see, and why? You do not
> have to like every detail.

Record the choice, the reason, time to a decision, and whether the participant
could tell the concepts apart without being told the direction names. If the
participant says they are “the same”, ask which parts feel different and log the
answer; do not teach the difference.

If a live, approved discovery session is available, start the timer when the
owner submits their website URL instead, and record the discovery segment
separately. Live crawling is not permitted in the current environment, so the
seeded mode is the only mode prepared here.

**Measure:** time from first concept view (or URL submit in live mode) to a
clear choice; number of previews opened; comprehension of the differences.

### Task 3 — Complete owner review

Ask the participant to use the project workspace and review page to:

- find the business details that need attention;
- add one missing contact detail that they know;
- confirm or correct one uncertain value;
- open the catalogue without help.

Use a test value supplied by the participant (for example their public business
phone), never a real customer record. Save it, then reload the page and confirm
it persisted.

**Measure:** time to finish; corrections made; wrong turns; help requests;
whether they can find the catalogue without being told it is behind a button.

### Task 4 — Make it publish-ready and preview independently

Ask:

> Make whatever changes you would need before showing this to a customer, then
> show me the customer app.

Observe without naming the Preview button. The participant should:

1. make at least one real edit;
2. save it;
3. open the customer app;
4. check the change on the customer screen;
5. say whether they would approve the build.

Do not ask them to press **Approve** in the pilot project. Approval is reserved
for a real owner sign-off, not a usability test.

**Measure:** time to “I would approve this”; whether the customer preview opened
without help; whether the saved change is visible; whether they understand that
the app is still private.

### Task 5 — Feedback (5 minutes)

Ask open questions, then read the statements below and record 1–5 agreement.

Open questions:

- What did you expect Plattr to know that it did not know?
- Where did you have to repeat something the website already told Plattr?
- Which words or screens felt technical?
- What would stop you from sending this app to a customer?
- What would you change first?

Statements (1 = strongly disagree, 5 = strongly agree):

1. I understood what Plattr found on my website.
2. I could tell what was missing or uncertain.
3. I knew where to edit each thing.
4. The three concepts were meaningfully different.
5. I could preview the customer app without help.
6. I would be comfortable showing this app to a customer after my edits.
7. I could run this without someone sitting next to me.

## Metrics

| Metric | Definition | Go/no-go threshold |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discovery → concept choice | URL submit to clear choice in live mode; first concept reveal to clear choice in seeded mode | median ≤ 8 min; all 4 choose |
| Owner review completion | review page open to “I have checked it” | median ≤ 12 min; 4/4 complete |
| Corrections required | saved field/catalogue changes plus repeated explanations | median ≤ 5; no correction caused by a technical label |
| Confusing steps | think-aloud confusion, wrong destination, or repeated attempt | ≤ 2 per participant; no same blocker in 3 sessions |
| Abandoned steps | participant stops or asks moderator to do it | 0 critical tasks abandoned |
| Publish-ready time | concept choice to “I would approve this” | median ≤ 25 min |
| Independent edit + preview | save, reload, open customer app unaided | 4/4 |
| Trust | post-session statement 6 | median ≥ 4 |

These thresholds are hypotheses for the first cohort. If two owners hit the same
blocker, stop and fix it before the next session; do not average it away.

## Moderator intervention log

Record every intervention with:

- task number;
- what the participant tried;
- the exact words of the question;
- what the moderator did (pointed, named a label, took the mouse);
- whether the participant could continue afterwards.

No intervention is “minor” if it reveals that the interface did not explain
itself.

## Analysis

- Report each participant separately before aggregating.
- Separate **comprehension** failures from **visual** and **technical** failures.
- Quote the participant's words for the three most important issues.
- Count only tasks completed without intervention as independent.
- Do not call a session successful because the app rendered or the browser
  automation passed. The evidence is the owner's unaided behaviour.

## Stop conditions

Pause the pilot and escalate if:

- an owner cannot tell what is missing or where to edit it;
- an owner believes the private preview is already public;
- an owner cannot preview the customer app after an edit;
- two owners abandon the same task;
- an owner sees invented content, a wrong price or a wrong contact detail;
- the owner key or another owner's project is exposed.

## Output files

- `owner-usability-log.csv` — one row per participant.
- session notes, with secrets and customer data removed;
- screenshots only with consent;
- a short findings memo that separates observed behaviour from moderator
  interpretation;
- a decision: continue, fix and retest, or stop.

## What this test does not cover

- Real customer traffic or payment.
- iOS/Android installation on physical devices.
- Production backups, monitoring, deletion or incident response.
- Whether an owner will pay.
- More than four owners in the first cohort.
